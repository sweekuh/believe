# Season 3 season review (2026-10-12)

This is the same whole-season pass as `s4-season-review.md`, run on Season 3. Season 3
aired in 2023, so the commentary pass covered 2023 reviews and later retrospectives. It
also checked how Season 4 (2026) recasts Season 3. Each changed card carries a
`Season review 2026-10-12:` line in its `groundingNotes`.

## Method

- Every card was read end to end.
- Six researchers each took two episodes. Each one:
  - rebuilt the on-screen beat order from fan transcripts (Forever Dreaming), Wikipedia and
    recaps;
  - fact-checked every card;
  - ran a commentary pass.
- Some sites could not be reached:
  - Reddit, the Fandom wiki, TV Tropes and Tunefind episode pages were blocked or
    paywalled.
  - Many earlier Season 3 cards rested on search snippets alone. Those were re-checked
    against opened pages where possible.

## What the commentary says

- **2023 reception.**
  - E3–E5 were well liked.
  - E9 split critics: the A.V. Club gave it a C and Vulture 2/5, while the NYT liked it.
  - E10 sits at 60% on Rotten Tomatoes ("disjointed").
  - The finale scored 75% ("steady, if somewhat uneventful"). Critics called it touching
    and overlong, a good end to a weak season.
- **Most-praised moments:**
  - Roy's E9 press conference.
  - The E4 Grant Wahl tribute.
  - Jamie wearing Sam's number (E10).
  - The rebuilt BELIEVE sign and the goldfish ending (E12).
- **2026 lookbacks.** The Athletic called Season 4's finale a "do-over" of this one. Collider
  says the S3 finale is the series' top-rated episode on IMDb; that is single-source.

## Factual errors corrected

- **E1**
  - Sharon's "doubt is removed by action" is **Thomas Carlyle**, from *Sartor Resartus*.
    The card had pointed at William James.
  - In this episode Nate spits under the press-conference table. The mirror version is
    Season 2.
  - Ted never draws the Pennywise parallel.
- **E2.** Rebecca's Zava pitch was not reverse psychology. She meant to charm him, ended up
  yelling, and thought she had failed.
- **E3**
  - Roy offers to train Jamie; Jamie does not ask.
  - The avocado farm belongs to E5, so mentioning it here also gave that episode away.
- **E4.** The biggest error of the season. Beard and Roy *do* show the squad the Nate
  footage, at half-time while Ted is out. Richmond get three red cards and lose 4–1, and Ted
  takes the drive afterwards.
- **E5**
  - The matchbook recognition is in E3.
  - E5's beat is Rebecca's fertility news, which had no card anywhere.
  - The panic and the news are separate scenes.
- **E6**
  - "Moved to tears" is unsupported.
  - In *Pulp Fiction*, "Waitin' in School" is sung live by a Ricky Nelson look-alike.
  - Ted Lasso the character was not created at Boom Chicago.
- **E7**
  - Sam's father comforts him in the dressing room, not the wrecked restaurant.
  - Ted's red-string version is the real Japanese telling of the legend, not a mistake.
  - The Total Football card now uses Beard's true 1974 World Cup final fact instead of
    repeating E6's history.
- **E8**
  - Ted's divorce was final in Season 1, so the Hey Jude card no longer says he is losing
    his marriage.
  - The Lennons had separated, not divorced, when McCartney wrote the song.
  - The locker-room sequence was reversed.
  - Jamie arrives the next morning.
- **E9**
  - Thomas Hitzlsperger played in the Premier League and came out after retiring. The
    accurate claim is that no man has played in the Premier League *while* openly gay.
  - Colin comes out to his team only.
- **E10**
  - Sam turned Akufo down in the Season 2 finale.
  - The bribe went to the Nigerian government, not the football authorities.
  - The real Super League was semi-closed.
- **E11**
  - Ted's advice at the match, not his mum's, sends Jamie back on.
  - Nate had already decided to return before Beard arrived.
  - Larkin's plea is spoken outright in the poem.
- **E12**
  - The number is sung for Ted *and* Beard, and Roy is the Julie Andrews fan.
  - "A farewell to the series" and "the show's ending" are out of date now that Season 4
    exists.
